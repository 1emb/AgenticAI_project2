Registrar schema

Twelve tables in registrar/models/, PostgreSQL 18. Natural keys throughout: a human is a university_id, a course is its catalog code, a term is its six-digit code, a lecture is term plus CRN. Example rows below are illustrative and consistent across tables; the database holds no data yet.

PK primary key FK foreign key, must match a row in the named table UNIQUE no two rows share the value Enum columns are VARCHAR with a CHECK constraint.

course_prerequisite

catalog.py

One row per (course, required course). A course with no rows has no prerequisites. A course cannot require itself (CHECK).

ColumnTypeKeyMeaning
course_codevarchar(10)PK FK courseThe course being entered
required_course_codevarchar(10)PK FK courseThe course that must be passed first
min_gradevarchar(2)FK grade_scaleDefault C-. Satisfied when the earned grade has at least this many points.
group_numberintegerDefault 1. Same group = OR; different groups = AND.

Example rows

Read: CS 3310 needs (CS 2210 or CS 2215, grade C- or better) and (MATH 1511, grade C or better). CS 2210 needs CS 1110. CS 1110 has no rows, so anyone can take it.

grades.py

Reference data: these 14 rows are inserted by the initial migration, not by the seed. course_prerequisite.min_grade points here, and so will the enrollment grade.

Read: "C- or better" means points of at least 1.7, so a B+ (3.3) passes. GPA is the sum of points times credit hours, divided by credit hours, over rows with points. A grade earns credit when points are above zero, so F, W and I earn nothing.
Not modeled yet: enrollment (which student sits in which lecture, with what grade). Without it, "what have I taken", "can I add" and every statistic have nothing to query. Source of truth: registrar/models/*.py and the schema section of README.md.
